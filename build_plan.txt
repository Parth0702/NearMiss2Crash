Overview

This plan maps every file in the project to the specific research paper(s) it should draw on,
week by week. For each file: the paper(s) to feed Cursor, and the exact knowledge/technique
to pull from that paper. Work through this in order -- later weeks depend on decisions made
in Week 1.
Priority if time runs short: protect Weeks 1-2 (data + baseline). Cut Week 3's SafeAer-
oBERT / LLM-CoT additions before cutting anything earlier.

Week 1 -- Data Foundation & Labeling (Stage 0)

Day 1-2: data_loading.py

Papers: Liu, Li, Ng, Han & Feng (2025) -- "Accident investigation via LLMs reasoning:
HFACS-guided Chain-of-Thoughts" (Section 4, GAHFACS dataset) + your own Project Plan
document.
What to feed Cursor:

    · How GAHFACS was actually built: filtered NTSB records to far-part = 091, matched
       "accident narratives" and "findings" tables via the ev-id column, used GPT-3.5 to extract
       witness statements from the narr_accp / narr_accf columns, then had two human experts
       recode findings into HFACS 8.0.

    · Implement load_ntsb() expecting this same raw structure (ev-id, narr_accp, narr_accf,
       findings table) if pulling directly from NTSB CAROL / avall.zip, rather than guessing
       column names.

    · Implement standardize_schema() mapping either raw ASRS or raw NTSB exports into
       the common schema: report_id, source, text, label.

Day 3-5: keyword_weak_labeler.py (new file)

Paper: Madeira, Melício, Valério & Santos (2021) -- "Machine Learning and NLP for Predic-
tion of Human Factors in Aviation Incident Reports" (Section 2.2, Table 1).
What to feed Cursor:

    · The consistency criterion, verbatim: for a candidate keyword, sample 15 random docu-
       ments containing it. If  12 of 15 agree on one HFACS-ML class, label all documents

Day 6-7: weak_labeler_cluster.py (new file, optional second labeler)

Paper: Macedo, Ramos, Maior, Moura & Lins (2026) -- "Automated identification of pilot
failures in aviation accidents using a BERT-based classifier and topic modeling" (Section 3.2,
Fig. 4-5, Table 2).
What to feed Cursor:

    · Their 3-step pipeline: (1) BERT embeddings, mean-pooled per document, (2) UMAP di-
       mensionality reduction, (3) HDBSCAN clustering (no predefined cluster count)  class-
       based TF-IDF to name each cluster with its top 5 words.

    · Their hyperparameter sensitivity analysis: vary min_cluster_size and min_samples,
       track % of unassigned outliers, and pick the config balancing outlier rate against cluster
       coherence -- replicate this as a small grid search so the choice of HDBSCAN parameters
       is defensible.

    · Output cluster  keyword tables so you can manually assign each cluster to one of the
       4 HFACS classes, saved to a JSON config file.

Week 2 -- Baseline Pipeline & Cross-Domain Test (Stage 1)

Day 8-9: preprocessing.py

Papers: Madeira et al. (2021), Section 2.3 + Shi, Zurada, Cao & Guan (2022), Section 3.1.
What to feed Cursor:

    · Madeira's 3-stage pipeline: (1) cleaning -- remove duplicates, remove terrorism-caused
       incidents as non-representative of normal human error; (2) normalization -- translate
       non-English text, lowercase, strip punctuation; (3) tokenization -- alphabetic-only pars-
       ing (strip digits), remove a standard stopword list and a domain-specific list (summary,
       probable, cause, accident, contribute, factor, find, conclusion, ...), then
       lemmatize, then drop words appearing  5 times corpus-wide.

    · Implement this exact order (clean  normalize  tokenize  dual stopword removal 
       lemmatize  rare-word pruning) since it's a documented, citable choice.

Day 10-12: baseline_models.py

Papers: Madeira et al. (2021), Sections 3.1-3.3 + Shi et al. (2022), Section 3.1.
What to feed Cursor:

    · The TF-IDF formula and the Word2Vec / Doc2Vec alternatives from Madeira Section 3.
       Add a config flag FEATURE_METHOD = "tfidf" | "word2vec" | "doc2vec" to swap extrac-
       tors -- mirrors the real ablation these papers ran.

Day 13-14: evaluation.py

Papers: Vempati, Woods & Solano (2023), Table 1 + Madeira et al. (2021), Section 4.2 (Eq.
7-9).
What to feed Cursor:

    · Madeira's exact metric set: Micro-F1 as the primary metric, Macro-F1 and Precision
       as secondary. Implement all three -- accuracy alone is misleading under class imbalance.

    · The cross-domain sanity check: after evaluation, print the ASRS-vs-NTSB per-class distri-
       bution and compare against Vempati's published ASRS numbers (Perceptual 53%, Deci-
       sion 32%, Skill-Based 26%; CRM precondition 53%). Run this automatically right before
       the ASRS  NTSB transfer test, and log it to a results file.

Week 3 -- Transformer Models & Extra Baselines (Stage 2)

Day 15-17: transformer_model.py

Papers: Andrade & Walsh (2023) -- "SafeAeroBERT" (Section IV) + your original DistilBERT
spec.
What to feed Cursor:

    · SafeAeroBERT's setup: further pre-trained on 262,935 ASRS + 161,460 NTSB reports;
       fine-tuned via binary relevance (one dense layer per label, binary cross-entropy per
       class) rather than a single softmax multi-class head. Add a BASE_CHECKPOINT config
       switch (DistilBERT vs. SafeAeroBERT's public checkpoint) using the same fine-tuning
       loop.

    · Their reported pattern -- SafeAeroBERT beat base BERT/SciBERT on weather/procedure
       classification but not on human-factors/aircraft classification. Structure output tables
       the same way (per-class precision/recall/F1, not just aggregate accuracy) so it's visible
       where domain pretraining actually helps.

Day 18-19: llm_cot_baseline.py (new file)

Paper: Liu, Li, Ng, Han & Feng (2025), Section 3.2-3.3 + Appendix C/D (working code).
What to feed Cursor:

    · Their Table 1 question set directly: Q1 -- did the mishap individual do/fail to do some-
       thing?  Q2 -- performance/skill error?  Q3 -- judgment/decision error?  Q4 --
       known deviation (only asked if Q2 and Q3 are both "no"). Their Eq. 4 logic for resolving
       overlapping answers into one label.

    · Use their HFACS-CoT+ variant specifically (not HFACS-CoT): ask each question as a
       separate LLM call rather than all at once -- their ablation (Table 4) showed this sequen-
       tial approach fixes logical inconsistencies and lifts F1/recall substantially over a single
       combined prompt.

Day 20-21: model_comparison.py (new file)

Papers: All three model papers above, combined.
What to feed Cursor:

    · One unified comparison function that every model family (TF-IDF+LogReg, TF-
       IDF+SVM, DistilBERT, SafeAeroBERT, LLM-CoT+) reports into: columns = model |
       domain (in/cross) | Micro-F1 | Macro-F1 | Precision | Recall. This becomes the master

Week 4 -- Explainability, Tuning, Phase Analysis, Writeup
(Stage 3-4)

Day 22-23: explainability.py

Paper: Andrade & Walsh (2023), Section IV.D -- context only, no new technique.
What to feed Cursor:

    · Make sure SHAP runs against whichever transformer is currently active (DistilBERT or
       SafeAeroBERT via the config flag).

    · Keep the "top-weighted words per class" method for LogReg as the simpler comparison
       point (already in the original plan).

Day 24-25: hyperparameter_tuning.py (new file)

Paper: Madeira et al. (2021), Sections 4.4-4.5, Table 5, Figures 7-9.
What to feed Cursor:

    · Their random-search ranges as a template (train size 0.2-0.8, learning rate 0.0025-0.25
       log-uniform, epochs 1-100 log-uniform, etc.) -- adapt ranges to your SVM / TF-IDF /
       DistilBERT hyperparameters instead of their LS/D2V ones.

    · Their fANOVA finding: only 2-3 of 7 hyperparameters actually mattered. Compute and
       print a similar importance ranking (fANOVA if available, else a variance contribution
       proxy) after ~30-50 random search trials.

    · Their finding that Bayesian optimization gains plateaued/reversed past 4 free variables
       -- worth using as a stopping rule if Bayesian opt is also tried.

Day 26-27: flight_phase_analysis.py (Stage 4 bonus)

Paper: Vempati, Woods & Solano (2023), Table 2 + Figures 4-7.
What to feed Cursor:

    · Their weighted keyword list as the seed phase/environment keyword set: runway
       (2.96%), aircraft (1.55%), landing (1.18%), right (1.18%), airport (1.0%), approach
       (0.96%), left (0.96%), pattern (0.86%), around / ctaf / tower / winds (0.73% each).
       Extend with standard phase vocabulary: taxi, takeoff, climb, cruise, descent, final,
       go-around, touchdown.

Day 28-30: Cross-domain discussion & final report

Papers: Vempati, Woods & Solano (2023) Discussion section + Liu et al. (2025) Section 5.4 /
5.6 -- for your own writing, not Cursor.

What to use yourself:

       relative to NTSB's externally investigated ground truth.

       paring your LLM-CoT baseline's per-class F1 against human-only judgment.
